# WITH. Master Brand Guide

> Canonical brand system for WITH., the South African personal safety network concept.

**Public brand:** `WITH.`  
**Repository / domain identifier:** `withhelp`  
**Primary domain:** `withhelp.co.za`  
**Brand stage:** Research, prototype and pilot preparation  
**Brand status:** Working identity. Trademark, company-name and final legal clearance are still required before permanent commercial rollout.

# 1. Brand in one page

## Brand name

**WITH.**

Always written in uppercase with the full stop when used as the brand mark or formal brand name.

The full stop matters. It makes the name feel complete, calm and intentional.

## What WITH. is

WITH. is a personal safety network being designed to connect the things people already use into one protection loop: phones, watches, trusted people, transport, nearby staffed businesses, professional responders and existing emergency services.

## What WITH. is not

WITH. is not another panic button, a live crime feed, a surveillance product, a security company control panel, a partner-tracking tool, or an AI that claims to predict violence.

## Primary brand line

> **Safety should start before the emergency.**

This is the master line for the brand.

It should appear on the homepage, launch material, investor or partner introductions, product education and brand storytelling.

## Product promise

> **Get me from A to B with someone or something watching, and get me to help quickly if something goes wrong.**

This is the clearest functional expression of what the product is trying to achieve.

## Core brand idea

> **Safety, with you.**

This is the shortest emotional expression of the brand.

Use it selectively. It works well in campaign, packaging, event and social contexts, but the primary brand line remains **Safety should start before the emergency.**

## Brand belief

> People should not have to become security experts to move through ordinary life.

## Brand principle

> **No single failure should end the safety chain.**

## Product principle

> **An incident must survive the phone.**

## Brand character

Calm. Human. Credible. Prepared. Protective. Clear. Quietly capable.

## Visual character

Warm cream. Near black. Signal coral. Protective lime. Strong typography. Generous spacing. Real people. Ordinary environments. Very little visual fear.

# 2. The meaning of the name

## Why WITH.

The name is intentionally relational.

Safety is rarely one object. It is a network of people, places, devices and systems acting together.

WITH. means:

- with your people
- with nearby help
- with the devices you already use
- with a trusted business when you need somewhere to go
- with a professional responder when available
- with a system that keeps going when one layer fails
- with support after an incident, not only during it

The name shifts the emotional frame from fear to accompaniment.

The user should not feel that WITH. is watching them all the time.

The user should feel that WITH. is available when they choose to activate protection.

## WITH. versus withhelp

Use **WITH.** for the public-facing product and brand.

Use **withhelp** for infrastructure and administrative contexts such as:

- `withhelp.co.za`
- GitHub repository `Phuturedigital/withhelp`
- technical project names
- internal documentation
- email domains if adopted
- legal or company naming if required

Do not routinely present **WithHelp** as the consumer brand unless legal or product constraints eventually require it.

## How to say it

Say it naturally as **“with”**.

Do not pronounce the full stop.

# 3. Mission, vision, purpose and ambition

## Mission

> **Build a personal safety network that starts before an emergency, continues when individual systems fail, and helps people reach real human support when they need it.**

A fuller version:

WITH. exists to reduce the burden placed on individuals to coordinate their own protection in difficult moments. We aim to connect the systems people already use into a more continuous safety layer that can prepare, guard, notice, verify, act, reach and recover without requiring one device, one person, one network or one provider to carry the entire responsibility.

## Vision

> **A world where ordinary movement does not require constant fear, and where access to meaningful safety support is part of everyday infrastructure.**

South African expression:

> We want personal safety to become part of the infrastructure of everyday South African life, not a privilege activated only after something has already gone wrong.

## Purpose

> **Give people more continuity, choice and support around vulnerable moments.**

## Long-term ambition

WITH. can become a distributed safety layer that connects:

- individuals
- families and trusted people
- employers
- universities
- banks and insurers
- property groups
- transport platforms
- staffed businesses
- private security
- medical response
- community organisations
- public emergency services
- recovery and support services

The long-term ambition is not to own every part of the safety system.

The ambition is to make the parts work together more reliably.

# 4. The problem WITH. is responding to

## The wrong framing

The problem is not simply:

> There are not enough safety apps.

There are already panic buttons, location-sharing tools, emergency numbers, security companies, watches, messaging apps, community groups, transport platforms and public services.

## The WITH. framing

> **Safety infrastructure exists, but it is fragmented.**

People often have to coordinate it themselves while stressed, uncertain or already in danger.

They may need to:

- recognise that something is wrong
- unlock a phone
- find the right app
- know which number to call
- explain where they are
- hope the right person sees a message
- know which security company covers the area
- trust that a nearby business actually knows how to help
- hope one network connection remains available

WITH. exists to reduce that coordination burden.

## The deeper problem

Many safety systems begin only after the user can actively ask for help.

WITH. is built around the idea that useful protection can begin earlier.

The system can prepare before a journey, remain aware during a protected moment, check when an expected pattern breaks, and continue escalation even when one component disappears.

# 6. Audience

WITH. has one consumer brand but several participant groups.

## 6.1 Individuals

Primary need:

> Help me feel more prepared and less alone during vulnerable moments without making me feel constantly watched.

Examples:

- walking to a car
- travelling after dark
- taking an Uber, Bolt or taxi
- commuting
- arriving home
- moving through a campus
- working late
- concern involving someone known
- needing a staffed place to go

What they need to hear:

- simple
- optional
- user controlled
- private
- useful before danger
- still useful if a phone or connection fails
- no permanent tracking by default

## 6.2 Businesses / Assistance Points

Primary need:

> Give my staff a clear, safe procedure for helping someone without turning them into security officers.

What they need to hear:

- no confrontation
- no map of nearby users
- simple staff process
- status can be marked unavailable
- professional response remains external
- verification is operational, not a marketing sticker

## 6.3 Employers

Primary need:

> Support staff who travel early, late or through vulnerable environments without becoming an employer surveillance system.

What they need to hear:

- employee-controlled protection
- sponsored access
- aggregate reporting
- privacy boundaries
- after-hours duty-of-care support
- no continuous employee tracking

## 6.4 Universities and campuses

Primary need:

> Connect students, campus security, transport and staffed help points into a clearer protection network.

What they need to hear:

- campus Assistance Points
- Guardian for movement between buildings, transport and residences
- privacy preserved
- institution does not automatically see individual movement
- sponsorship can remove cost barriers

## 6.5 Banks and insurers

Primary need:

> Add useful prevention and protection infrastructure around customers without pretending safety is guaranteed.

What they need to hear:

- sponsored access
- optional response cover
- measurable protected activity
- privacy-safe aggregate insight
- potential reduction of friction after incidents
- no sale of individual movement histories

## 6.6 Property groups

Primary need:

> Turn buildings, precincts and staffed sites into a connected assistance network.

## 6.7 Professional responders

Primary need:

> Receive better incident context earlier, with measurable acknowledgement and handoff states.

## 6.8 GBV organisations, NGOs and support organisations

Primary need:

> Ensure the product supports real-world recovery and known-person risk without replacing specialist human services.

# 8. Brand values

## 8.1 Continuity

Protection should not collapse because one thing failed.

## 8.2 Agency

The user chooses when protection is active, who receives information and what is shared.

## 8.3 Privacy

Privacy is part of the safety architecture, not a policy page added later.

## 8.4 Practical help

The product must connect to real people, real places and real response paths.

## 8.5 Clarity

In a stressful moment the interface should say what is happening, what has happened and what comes next.

## 8.6 Accessibility

Core safety should aim to remain available regardless of income, device choice or technical sophistication.

## 8.7 Humility

Never claim more safety, response speed, prediction or coverage than has actually been proven.

## 8.8 Recovery

Safety does not end when the immediate incident ends.

# 11. Messaging hierarchy

## 5-second explanation

> **WITH. connects your phone, trusted people, nearby help and professional response into one personal safety network.**

## 15-second explanation

> **WITH. is a personal safety network designed to start before an emergency. You can protect a journey or short vulnerable moment, keep trusted people ready, find verified places that know how to help, and escalate if something goes wrong.**

## 30-second explanation

> **Most safety tools start after you press a panic button. WITH. starts earlier. It is being designed to connect your phone, watch, Trusted Circle, transport, nearby Assistance Points and professional responders into one protection loop. If one layer fails, the rest of the safety chain should be able to continue.**

## 2-minute explanation

> **WITH. is being built as a personal safety network rather than another emergency button. The idea is simple: before a journey or vulnerable moment, you can start protection. The phone can monitor basic expected patterns locally, such as route progress or a Safety Countdown, while the backend keeps durable state. If something meaningfully changes, the system can check in and, where configured, escalate to your Trusted Circle and a professional response partner. Nearby verified Assistance Points can give you a staffed place to reach. Private Risk supports situations involving someone known without labelling the relationship or secretly tracking another person. The entire system is designed around continuity, privacy and real-world handoffs.**

# 13. Voice and tone

## Voice principles

### 13.1 Calm

Use steady language.

Bad:

> DANGER DETECTED. YOU MAY BE AT RISK.

Better:

> Something changed. Are you okay?

### 13.2 Plain

Avoid security jargon unless speaking to professional responders.

Bad:

> Multi-channel incident orchestration initiated.

Better:

> Help flow started.

### 13.3 Transparent

Say what the system can and cannot do.

Good:

> Your phone is offline. Guardian is continuing locally. Your last confirmed server state was 2 minutes ago.

### 13.4 Respectful

Do not infantilise users.

Avoid:

> Don’t worry, we’ve got you.

Prefer:

> Guardian is active. Your Trusted Circle is ready if the safety flow escalates.

### 13.5 Non-judgmental

Private Risk should never tell someone what their relationship means.

### 13.6 South African, not forced

Use local context, services, places and realities naturally.

Do not insert slang simply to sound local.

# 17. Logo system

## Master wordmark

**WITH.**

Recommended treatment:

- uppercase
- heavy weight
- tight tracking
- near-black letters
- coral full stop

Example concept:

`WITH` in near black + `.` in signal coral.

## Clear space

Minimum clear space should equal the approximate diameter of the full stop around all sides of the wordmark.

For high-visibility partner usage, increase clear space to 2× the full stop.

## Minimum size

Digital wordmark should not be used so small that the full stop is visually lost.

If the logo becomes too small, use a simplified approved mark rather than squeezing the wordmark.

## Future app icon direction

Preferred concept:

- near-black field or warm cream field
- one clear coral full stop / signal mark
- optional abstract “W” only if recognisable at small sizes

Avoid:

- shields
- padlocks
- weapons
- sirens
- police badges
- crosses that imply medical service
- generic location-pin logos

The brand should not look like a security company.

## Logo misuse

Do not:

- remove the full stop in the formal logo
- turn the logo red
- add gradients to the wordmark
- put crime imagery inside the mark
- stretch or condense the logo
- add shadows or glow
- add slogans inside the logo lockup unless using an approved campaign layout

# 18. Typography

## Primary typeface

**Inter**

Reason:

- highly legible
- neutral without feeling cold
- strong digital performance
- broad language support
- reliable across web and product

## Weight system

- 900: major brand statements and logo-style display
- 800: key actions and section headings
- 700: UI emphasis
- 500: supporting emphasis
- 400: body copy

## Display treatment

Headlines should be:

- large
- tightly tracked
- short
- visually confident
- usually sentence case rather than all caps

Example:

> Safety should start before the emergency.

## UI treatment

UI text should favour legibility over brand drama.

Important incident information should never use decorative typography.

## Fallback stack

`Inter, -apple-system, BlinkMacSystemFont, "Segoe UI", sans-serif`

# 26. AI brand behaviour

AI should behave like an educator and guide, not an oracle.

## Ask WITH. role

Ask WITH. can:

- explain features
- explain what happens if a phone is lost
- explain privacy
- explain Assistance Points
- help prepare a personal safety plan
- explain organisational participation
- explain what a protected journey does

## Ask WITH. must not

- predict assault
- tell a user that a partner is abusive
- assess guilt
- diagnose mental state
- claim to know whether the user is currently safe
- replace emergency services
- create fake risk scores

## Immediate-danger behaviour

When someone indicates immediate danger, product education stops.

The interface should prioritise existing human emergency options and clear actions.

# 40. Brand governance

## Every new feature should pass these questions

1. Does this increase real protection or only perceived sophistication?
2. Does it preserve user agency?
3. Could it be used for stalking or coercion?
4. Does it create unnecessary location retention?
5. Does it still work if one vendor fails?
6. Does the copy overclaim what is technically proven?
7. Does it make the product feel more frightening than useful?
8. Can someone understand it under stress?
9. Is it still useful without AI?
10. Is it still useful without a smartwatch?

## Every campaign should pass these questions

1. Are we using fear to manufacture urgency?
2. Are all numbers and partner claims verifiable?
3. Are we implying guaranteed safety?
4. Are we showing ordinary life rather than rehearsing violence?
5. Would a survivor or someone in Private Risk find this respectful?
6. Is the call to action clear?

# 41. Brand decision hierarchy

When brand choices conflict, use this order:

1. **Safety and truthful communication**
2. **Privacy and user agency**
3. **Clarity under stress**
4. **Reliability**
5. **Accessibility**
6. **Brand consistency**
7. **Aesthetic preference**

A beautiful brand choice never overrides a safety requirement.

# 44. Brand success criteria

The brand is working when people understand:

- WITH. starts before an emergency
- it is more than an SOS button
- Guardian is optional and user controlled
- Assistance Points are verified processes, not guaranteed safe zones
- Trusted Circle is not permanent tracking
- one lost phone should not end the incident
- the product connects existing systems rather than trying to replace all of them
- the brand is serious without making everyday life feel frightening

The brand is failing when people assume:

- WITH. is a security company
- WITH. monitors everyone all the time
- WITH. predicts attacks using AI
- Assistance Points guarantee safety
- the app requires a smartwatch
- the product only works for strangers attacking people in public
- the brand sells fear
